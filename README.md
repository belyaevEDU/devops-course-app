# Script

Imagine this. The year is 2010, the date is August 24th. You boot up this indie game that you wanted to try out called Minecraft. You create a new world in single-player. Your computer isn’t specifically great, so you’re playing on low render distance. Everything seems to be normal, you begin chopping down trees and crafting a workbench. You notice something move amongst the dense fog. You think it was a cow, so you go at it, as you need leather for armor. But nothing was there. You forget it and continue playing the game, but as you expand the world you see things that seem out of place. Tunnels in stone, small sand pyramids in the ocean and trees stripped of their leaves. Looking back, it came out to be a strange entity, a player, and his name is Herobrine.

## Origins

Herobrine has been around for almost 13 years and he sure didn’t come out of nowhere. His story originates from another creepypasta, called “White Eyes”. A month before the Herobrine post was published, a user by the name Flaky posted a comment on Minecraft Forums, a copied 4chan post about the story of a player encountering an entity with glowing white eyes after listening to the “13” music disc. One alleged instance of those eyes appearing was described in a comment under the [youtube upload of](https://youtu.be/2cFpTSQDyzo) the music disc. The video itself contained a screenshot of a dark cave with two white eyes. Later, an altered version of the story appeared, in which “White Eyes” gained the name “Herobrine” and was now described as the deceased brother of Notch, pictured as Steve, just with, you guessed it, white eyes.

## Community response

### Brocraft

The community’s response was massive. The first people to fake more sightings were Brocraft streamers Copeland and Patimuss. Copeland began by editing Herobrine into several screenshots and sharing them with his chat. Upon receiving largely good reactions, he decided to take it further: stage a hoax. In one of the rooms in his house, he set a painting, which was retextured as Herobrine. Then, he went live. On the livestream, Copeland played through the world just as usual, avoiding that exact room. About 20 minutes and 45 seconds in, he entered the house and saw the re-textured painting. Copeland screamed and ran out of the room, ending the stream right after. 

Later on, the streamer Patimuss went live, showing Herobrine floating above a lava pool. Patimuss ran away, saved his game and turned his stream off. The stream resumed 10 minutes later, and by then, Herobrine had already disappeared. During the next stream, Patimuss could be heard talking to his wife, stating he was trolling, which flooded the livestream chat with outrage. He then faked a game crash and the stream proceeded later with no further Herobrine sightings. Needless to say, that Copeland did not like that Patimuss outed Herobrine as fake.

### After Brocraft

After Brocraft, the community continued to make tons of Herobrine-related content. Maps, videos, even songs. In December of the same year, hMod Forums user tkelly released the earliest known Herobrine mod, a server plugin titled "iLoveYou". 

## Mojang’s response

As per Mojang’s responses, at Minecon 2010 Notch confirmed, that Herobrine was a hoax, but jokingly said that he might not be. The next day, Notch replied to a tweet, asking if Herobrine was an entity in-game, saying that it was not, but that “it might be soon”.

Other than that, Herobrine has been mentioned to be removed from the game in the Minecraft’s changelogs. The first mention was in Beta 1.6.6. After that, most Minecraft changelogs included the entry “Removed Herobrine”. As of now, the latest mention of Herobrine was in the changelogs of 1.20.

## Finding the seed

Rolling into 2020, a community started forming around seedfinding. In the start of the year, SalC1 uploaded a video titled “The Mystery of the Minecraft pack.png File”, where he called it upon the community to help him find the seed for pack.png, the icon of the default resourcepack. In September, the seed was found with the help of Minecraft@Home and these people on the screen. In the meanwhile, the seed for the Minecraft title screen was found, as well as the seed for the “Skull on Fire” painting.

After the pack.png project, the community wanted to take on a new project, and that came out to be the original Herobrine screenshot seed. But how? Well, let’s take a good look at the screenshot.  

In front of us is a hill, two big trees, 8 small trees, a chicken and Herobrine. The last two will not be of any use, let’s talk about all of the other parts. 

The hill is pretty useful, due to a thing called the “quads” being visible. Those are terrain anomalies, always occurring at x/z coords which are multiples of four, reducing the number of the possible chunks from 256 down to 16.

The two big trees are a crucial part in finding the seed, as when compared to terrain, big tree seeds can be tested significantly faster. The eight small trees are also useful, as a big tree and a small tree cannot be in the same chunk, eliminating the half of possible chunks, reducing the number down to 8.

What else? Well, besides terrain, there are grass blocks. Their texture tells us that the player is facing the -x direction.

The next part is recreating the scenery  from the screenshot. A member of the team by the name Tomlacko [created an overlay](https://codepen.io/Tomlacko/full/PoNENLJ) to compare the original Herobrine screenshot and the world recreation. That was done to get a list of chunk-based coordinates that match the positions of the leaves on the two big trees. Even though not all leaves are seen on the screenshot, the information given  was enough to yield 430 million seeds to be tested. 

There’s a lot more of difficult math behind this, so I’ll leave a link in the description to the website of the project, as well as the google doc describing all of the methodology behind it.

So on January 16th, 2021, around 8:21pm eastern, the seed was found by andrew_555, a member of the Minecraft@Home team. But that’s not all that was found.

## Copeland World Found

In 2020, a YouTuber by the name Enderboss25 started a search for the infamous Copeland livestream. At first, he tried the web archive, but it didn’t save video. Next, he tried contacting livestream, the service that Copeland used to broadcast and it actually still exists. Enderboss got a response, saying that they don’t own the content and cannot release it without the owners’ permission.

Apparently, Brocraft had a website set up. An archive of it had lots of information, including links to the groups’ social media and Copelands’ steam group. Through an archive of the steam group, Enderboss grabbed Copeland’s Steam ID and found his steam account. To his luck, it was active and Enderboss actually managed to get in contact with him. Copeland was down to help.

The problem was that at the time Copeland wasn’t really active or vocal on the internet, at least under that username. But there was still hope. 

Copeland emailed Livestream to prove it was him and asked for the stream to be recovered. A few days later, he got an email, saying that the stream was deleted from their servers a long time ago. And it’s to no surprise, back then storage wasn’t cheap.

As per Copeland’s personal archive, he actually managed to find some of his old livestreams, but he didn’t find the one they were looking for.

It seemed like everything was lost. But the livestream wasn’t the only thing that could be found.

Copeland was searching though his computer and found some screenshots of the world the infamous livestream happened in. He was getting nostalgic, saying that he wished he still had copies of his old worlds. A few days later, Copeland found his old PC in his closet. Enderboss didn’t think much of it, until this happened. Copeland actually found the Minecraft world, in which the Herobrine livestream went down. And it’s safe to say, that that world is a huge part of Minecraft history. It’s what basically made Herobrine the legend that it is today.

Seeing these things resurface in the community is truly amazing. And I think it’s safe to say that one of the oldest Minecraft mysteries has been solved. Thank you for watching.
